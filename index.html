§01

Introduction

Most machine-learning projects die in a notebook. Mine are built to leave it.

I'm Shre Hari, and I've just finished an MSc in Artificial Intelligence at the University of Sheffield. What I care about is the unglamorous gap between a promising model and a system people actually rely on. At NexCell Solutions I work on the live side of that gap: a WhatsApp AI agent that talks to real customers and can only act through tools that are tested, idempotent and logged with care. Before that I built production backends at Hamiltonian Systems that moved 100,000+ records a week, and I'm first author of a peer-reviewed IEEE paper on real-time disease monitoring (CCPIS 2025).

In October I gave myself two weeks to build an agent the way production systems are built, and shipped AgentForge to Google Kubernetes Engine. The prompts took an afternoon. The queue, checkpoints, leases, sweeper, graceful shutdown and evals took the rest, and that's the part I'm proudest of.

My favourite results rarely come from swapping in a bigger model. They come from structured error analysis: finding that boundary truncation, not model capacity, was throttling an extractive QA system (F1 0.50 to 0.69), or that describing an image before scoring it beat asking CLIP directly by 30 points. Diagnosis before replacement. Evidence before opinion. Available immediately, full-time.

EXP.11 OCT 2026 LIVE ON GKE

AgentForge — a production AI agent, built solo in two weeks

A job-hunt agent. Sign in with Google, add a CV and a job ad, and it researches the company with live web search, rates the CV against every requirement (strong, partial or missing, with the CV line that backs it up), then drafts tailored CV bullets and the interview questions you're likely to face. It is the first agent on a low-code agent platform, and it's built to production standards rather than as a demo.

The hard part isn't the prompt. It's what happens when things go wrong: every run is queued on Pub/Sub, every step is checkpointed in Postgres, a crashed run picks up where it stopped, the one side effect can never happen twice, and a sweeper re-queues runs whose worker died. On shutdown, workers stop between steps and hand the run back, so a rolling deploy drops nothing.

  • WORKFLOWFive LangGraph steps (validate → research → match → draft → save) with a Postgres checkpoint after each. Crash and resume tested with a real process kill.
  • RESEARCHA Deep Agents step with Tavily search, a 3-search cap enforced in code, today's date in the prompt, and invented URLs dropped.

EXP.08 2026 MSC DISSERTATION · SUBMITTED SEP 2026

Visualising Emotion — Can a Text-to-Image Model Draw a Feeling?

At Sheffield's Festival of the Mind 2024, members of the public typed how they felt and DALL·E drew each entry as a black-and-white line drawing. I built the pipeline that asks whether the drawing carries the feeling back, across 1,012 records. BLIP-VQA is never asked for an emotion, only five factual questions per drawing; BART-MNLI reads the emotion from that description; GoEmotions reads the words. Every score is tested against a 300-shuffle random-pairing null, with clustered-bootstrap intervals and Holm-Bonferroni across 36 comparisons.

⤷ Valence-sign agreement 64.0% against a 45.6% null (z +11.1). Describe-then-extract beat direct CLIP by 30.6 points. A blind 120-drawing check put the face rule's precision at 42.2%, reported rather than buried. · source

EXP.01 2025 IEEE · FIRST AUTHOR

Real-Time Disease Monitoring & Alert System

A public-health system that predicts disease-prone areas and warns people nearby. My part was the machine-learning pipeline: cleaning the health data, Random Forest and Logistic Regression risk classifiers, ARIMA trend forecasts, and the link to geospatial risk zones. Combining model predictions with rule-based checks cut false alerts by about 25%, and people inside a 400-metre red zone get an SMS warning.
